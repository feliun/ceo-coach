# Leadership Framework

This file defines the CEO operating model used by the performance scorecard and calendar audit. Customize it to match your leadership philosophy.

---

## Role Hats

A CEO wears multiple hats. The distribution of time across hats reveals whether you're operating at the right altitude.

| Hat | Description | Target Allocation |
|-----|-------------|-------------------|
| **Learner** | Reading, absorbing, connecting ideas | Always on (background) |
| **Architect** | Designing systems, processes, strategy | ~25% |
| **Coach** | Developing people, giving feedback, unblocking teams | ~25% |
| **Engineer** | Building tools, prototypes, technical foundations | ~25% |
| **Player** | Doing work someone else could do at 70% of your level | ≤25% |

**Key signal:** If Player consistently exceeds 25%, you're not delegating enough. If Architect is below 15%, you're not thinking strategically.

---

## Constraint Theory

At any given time, the business has ONE primary constraint — the bottleneck that limits growth. The CEO's highest-leverage activity is working directly on that constraint.

**Self-check questions:**
- What is the current #1 constraint?
- Am I actually working on it, or working around it?
- How much of my time this period was spent at the point of constraint?

---

## Delegation Philosophy

- If someone else could do it at 70% of your level, delegate it
- Coach outcomes, not methods
- Track recurring non-delegated items — three weeks in a row triggers a formal handoff
- Delegation failure is a system problem, not a willpower problem

---

## Strategic vs. Tactical

| Horizon | Type | Examples |
|---------|------|----------|
| 12+ months | Strategic | Market positioning, culture design, hiring strategy, partnerships |
| <12 months | Tactical | Feature decisions, client escalations, operational fixes |

A healthy split depends on business stage, but strategic thinking time should never be zero.
